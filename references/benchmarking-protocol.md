# Benchmarking protocol

## Purpose

Use benchmarking to identify the shared scientific features of successful reviews and to
test whether the proposed manuscript has a defensible contribution. Do not use it to copy
phrasing or figure composition.

## Select benchmark reviews

Choose three to six papers that collectively satisfy:

- relevant material system, mechanism, or application;
- strong journal and current relevance;
- clear review method or scope statement;
- substantive evidence tables or figures;
- visible critical synthesis rather than a paper-by-paper catalogue;
- accessible full text for complete inspection.

Avoid choosing all benchmarks from one research group or one publisher.

## Extract comparable fields

Populate `assets/benchmark-review-matrix.csv` with:

- citation and DOI;
- journal and year;
- exact review question;
- material, mechanism, outcome, and application coverage;
- search and screening transparency;
- section architecture;
- number and function of figures and tables;
- treatment of contradictory evidence;
- limitations and research agenda;
- distinctive strengths;
- practices not worth imitating.

## Test topic overlap

Classify overlap separately:

1. material overlap;
2. mechanism overlap;
3. outcome overlap;
4. application overlap;
5. date-range overlap;
6. methodological overlap;
7. synthesis-axis overlap.

A topic can remain novel despite material overlap if it contributes a new comparison unit,
evidence framework, dataset, mechanism reconciliation, scale-up analysis, or reproducibility
standard. “Recent progress” alone is weak novelty.

## Benchmark the completed manuscript

Score no arbitrary points. Instead, record `meets`, `partly meets`, or `does not meet` with a
short reason for:

- question clarity;
- search reproducibility;
- evidence traceability;
- cross-study comparison;
- mechanism restraint;
- visual evidence quality;
- table utility;
- gap specificity;
- conclusion proportionality;
- reference and permission integrity.

Revise the manuscript where a gap affects scientific credibility or reader utility.
