# Adversarial visual review protocol

Use this protocol after the scientific role, sources, caption claim, target width, and
acceptance criteria have been frozen.

## 1. Freeze the contract

Complete one row per figure in `assets/visual-acceptance-gate.csv` before examining the
final composition. Record:

- one primary scientific question;
- the caption claim and manuscript claims it must support;
- source-register rows and transformations;
- intended width in the target manuscript;
- minimum final-size type;
- expected panel density and reading order;
- hard blockers and positive acceptance criteria;
- author decisions that must not be reversed.

Do not change a criterion after seeing the figure merely to preserve a preferred design.
Change it only when the scientific question or journal specification changes, and record
the reason.

## 2. Inspect the embedded figure

Judge the figure after it is embedded and rendered in the complete manuscript. Inspect at
100% page size and, for raster integrity, at 200%.

Fail the figure when any of these blockers is present:

- a label, axis, legend, scale bar, or essential annotation is unreadable;
- a source boundary, panel, label, or photograph is incomplete;
- a panel or crop duplicates another figure without a documented necessity;
- a mechanism arrow lacks a traceable evidentiary basis;
- different studies are presented as one paired experiment;
- inherited panel letters conflict with the review's panel labels;
- transformations obscure or alter scientific meaning;
- permission or attribution remains unresolved;
- a prior author decision has been silently reversed;
- the caption cannot explain why every panel is present.

## 3. Limit panel-job density

Count scientific jobs, not only panels.

- Allow up to four simple observational panels when they form one direct comparison and
  remain readable.
- Prefer no more than two detailed panels when axes, legends, source annotations, or dense
  microstructural labels must remain legible.
- Split a figure when it mixes protocol, response, microstructure, mechanism, and field
  demonstration without a single explicit causal question.
- Do not reduce type or crop evidence to avoid a necessary split.

## 4. Run at most two revision rounds

Round 1:

1. list blockers first;
2. list no more than five high-value refinements;
3. revise only the evidence hierarchy, panel allocation, typography, labelling, spacing,
   caption, or lawful crop required by those findings;
4. render again in the manuscript.

Round 2:

1. re-evaluate against the original frozen criteria;
2. check consistency against the complete figure set;
3. record `pass`, `split`, `replace`, or `delete`.

If a blocker remains, do not add a third cosmetic loop. Split, replace, or delete.

## 5. Review the complete figure set

Check:

- unique scientific role for every figure;
- consistent panel-label position, size, and contrast;
- one restrained palette for original synthesis figures;
- complete source images and valid scale bars;
- readable final-size typography;
- no repeated crops or near-duplicate photographs;
- captions below figures with panel descriptions;
- no internal drafting notes or licence shorthand in body text;
- evidence density comparable with benchmark reviews;
- a deliberate balance of original synthesis and source-grounded observational evidence.

## 6. Preserve scientific-image integrity

Do not generate, reconstruct, extend, clean, or beautify experimental images. Do not use
generative fill, object removal, synthetic textures, or unreported contrast manipulation.
Allow only documented operations that preserve meaning and are permitted by the licence,
such as uniform cropping, resizing, and global tonal adjustment.

An AI-created illustration is not required. If generative assistance is used for
non-evidentiary style ideation, rebuild the final scientific schematic as an editable,
evidence-linked composition and disclose the assistance when journal policy requires it.
