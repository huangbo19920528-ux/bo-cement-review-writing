# Bo Cement Review Writing

Build a review as an auditable scientific synthesis, not a long catalogue of papers. Treat
topic novelty, evidence traceability, cross-study comparison, visual provenance, and
rendered-page quality as one connected workflow.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Never invent a reference, DOI, author, result, permission statement, or figure source.
2. Verify bibliographic identity against a DOI registry or publisher record before citation.
3. Distinguish a verified bibliographic object from a scientifically valid interpretation.
4. Do not target a reference or figure count as a substitute for coverage and synthesis.
5. Do not reproduce or adapt a published figure without a documented reuse basis.
6. Never upload an unpublished manuscript or reviewer file to external services without
   explicit authorization.
7. Keep numerical comparisons tied to dosage basis, water balance, mixture design, age,
   control, uncertainty, and test method.
8. Prefer critical conditional conclusions over universal optimum dosages or mechanisms.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the review gap before fixing the title

- Record the target journal, intended scientific level, material system, practical problem,
  date range, and desired article type.
- Search recent reviews and primary studies around the proposed concept.
- Build a topic-overlap matrix using
  [assets/benchmark-review-matrix.csv](assets/benchmark-review-matrix.csv).
- Separate overlap in material, mechanism, outcome, application, and review method.
- Retain the topic only if it adds a defensible synthesis axis, evidence framework,
  comparison unit, dataset, or research agenda.
- Write a one-sentence novelty claim that could survive peer review. If it depends only on
  鈥渕ore recent papers,鈥?narrow or redesign the topic.

Read [references/benchmarking-protocol.md](references/benchmarking-protocol.md) when
selecting benchmark reviews or testing topic novelty.

### 2. Benchmark successful reviews without imitating their weaknesses

- Select three to six strong reviews from relevant high-quality journals.
- Extract their question structure, section balance, evidence tables, figure functions,
  limitation language, and conclusion discipline.
- Compare the planned manuscript against shared successful features, not surface styling.
- Do not copy prose, panel composition, arbitrary scoring systems, or unattributed images.
- Treat bibliometric maps, word clouds, radar charts, and decorative workflows as optional;
  retain them only when they answer a defined question with an auditable method.

### 3. Build a traceable evidence library

- Record databases, complete queries, search dates, filters, screening decisions, and update
  dates.
- Use [assets/evidence-matrix.csv](assets/evidence-matrix.csv) for study-level extraction.
- Verify each DOI and normalize duplicate records before drafting.
- Classify each source as primary experiment, method/standard, review, modelling/LCA,
  field demonstration, or image-provenance record.
- Mark evidence directness: direct measurement, mechanism-supporting observation,
  triangulated inference, or speculation.
- Treat multiple papers from one research programme as connected evidence, not independent
  replication.
- Add references because they close a coverage or evidence gap. Do not add papers merely to
  reach 100, 150, or another attractive total.

Read [references/evidence-and-citation-protocol.md](references/evidence-and-citation-protocol.md)
before finalizing the library.

### 4. Design a question-led architecture

- Make every main section answer a scientific question.
- Give each subsection enough comparative evidence to justify its existence. Merge a thin
  subsection when it contains only one or two short paragraphs.
- Allocate approximate word, table, and figure budgets before drafting.
- Use this default architecture when appropriate:
  1. problem, gap, scope, and contribution;
  2. search and critical-appraisal method;
  3. material identity, delivery state, and processing;
  4. mechanisms across fresh, early-age, and hardened states;
  5. performance and boundary conditions;
  6. emerging material classes or application pathways;
  7. cross-study synthesis and design implications;
  8. scale-up, sustainability, reporting requirements, and research agenda;
  9. conclusions.
- Keep methods reproducible but proportionate. Do not label a narrative search 鈥渟ystematic鈥?  unless its protocol and reporting support that claim.

Read [references/manuscript-architecture.md](references/manuscript-architecture.md) when
building or shortening the outline.

### 5. Draft in claim鈥揺vidence units

- Start each paragraph with a scientific claim or comparison, then supply the evidence,
  boundary conditions, conflicts, and interpretation.
- Use author-led sentences when attribution matters, but avoid a repetitive list of
  鈥淴 et al. found鈥︹€?
- Avoid citation piles attached to a broad sentence. Split distinct claims and place each
  citation beside the statement it supports.
- Prefer primary evidence for causal and numerical claims; use reviews for orientation.
- State whether reported improvements use equal water, equal workability, nominal or total
  water-to-binder ratio, dry or suspension dosage, and matched processing.
- Separate observation from explanation. Use 鈥渋s consistent with鈥?when the mechanism is
  inferred rather than directly measured.
- Explain contradictory findings through material identity, suspension solids, carrier
  water, pore-solution stability, dispersion energy, admixture chemistry, air, curing, and
  test method before calling the literature inconsistent.
- Keep abstract, conclusions, tables, figures, and reference numbering synchronized.

### 6. Use tables as evidence maps

- Prefer one finding, comparison, limitation, or recommendation per row.
- Include a dedicated reference column when rows summarize published evidence.
- Separate reported data from the review authors鈥?interpretation.
- Include comparison variables that expose confounders: material class, dimensions, surface
  chemistry, delivery state, dry dosage, water treatment, mixing, binder, age, and outcome.
- Do not fill cells with qualitative scores unless the scoring rule and source evidence are
  explicit.

### 7. Build a visual evidence system

- Give every figure a job: define scope, show material identity, compare data, explain a
  mechanism, document a test or microstructure, demonstrate field scale, or synthesize a
  decision framework.
- Use original synthesis graphics for cross-study logic and reproduced/adapted published
  panels only when they add evidence unavailable from redrawing.
- Pair quantitative plots with real micrographs, test photographs, or field images when the
  pairing materially improves interpretation.
- Place `(a)`, `(b)`, and later panel labels consistently at the upper left. Put panel
  descriptions in the caption, not as large titles over the image.
- Use one coherent font, label size, border treatment, and colour logic across all figures.
- Never repeat the same image or crop in two figures.
- Remove incomplete labels, legacy panel letters, clipped legends, unreadable annotations,
  and meaningless text below an image.
- Keep scale bars and scientifically necessary annotations.
- Record every reused or adapted panel in
  [assets/figure-source-register.csv](assets/figure-source-register.csv).
- Prefer original or openly reusable field photographs. A visually appealing image does not
  justify uncertain copyright or scientific relevance.
- For a long critical review, 8鈥?2 substantive figures may be reasonable, but stop when each
  remaining figure would be decorative or redundant.

Read [references/figure-and-permission-protocol.md](references/figure-and-permission-protocol.md)
before inserting any external image. Validate the source register with:

```bash
python scripts/validate_figure_register.py assets/figure-source-register.csv
```

### 8. Synthesize across studies

- Compare material鈥搈ixture鈥損rocessing combinations rather than treating the paper as the
  comparison unit.
- Grade confidence using directness, measurement quality, outcome consistency, and
  independent replication.
- Do not convert qualitative judgement into a precise-looking radar or heatmap score unless
  the rule, inputs, and sensitivity are auditable.
- Identify stop criteria, failure modes, and boundary conditions, not only promising ranges.
- Separate laboratory feasibility, printer or process validation, structure-scale
  demonstration, and long-term field performance.
- For sustainability claims, report both absolute and performance-normalized functional
  units when rankings depend on the denominator.
- End with a staged research agenda whose deliverables would close the evidence gaps.

### 9. Compare the complete draft with benchmarks

- Re-run the benchmark matrix after the full draft exists.
- Compare section balance, evidence density, figure purpose, table usefulness, critical
  depth, and conclusion restraint.
- Remove generic background, repeated mechanisms, short orphan subsections, decorative
  figures, and prose that merely says more research is needed.
- Do not force the manuscript to look identical to a published review. Match its evidentiary
  discipline and reader utility.

### 10. Run scientific and visual audits

- Use [references/final-audit.md](references/final-audit.md) for the full audit.
- For a DOCX manuscript, run:

```bash
python scripts/audit_review_docx.py manuscript.docx
```

- Render the complete DOCX or PDF and inspect every page at readable resolution.
- Confirm sequential figure, table, equation, and reference numbering.
- Confirm every in-text citation resolves to one reference and every reference is cited.
- Check duplicate embedded images by hash and visually inspect suspicious near-duplicates.
- Inspect equations, subscripts, superscripts, symbols, units, legends, and scale bars.
- Check that figures and captions stay together and that tables remain readable.
- Reconfirm all externally sourced panels against the figure-source register.
- Correct every count in the abstract and methods after the final reference library changes.

## Deliverables

Unless the user requests otherwise, deliver:

1. a concise novelty and scope statement;
2. a question-led outline or revised manuscript;
3. the evidence and figure-source registers;
4. a benchmark-gap summary;
5. a final audit summary distinguishing verified facts from unresolved uncertainty;
6. a rendered, visually inspected DOCX or PDF when manuscript production is requested.

Do not expose internal reviewer files, confidential source documents, temporary render files,
or unpublished research data in the final deliverables.
